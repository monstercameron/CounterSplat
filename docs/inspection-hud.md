# Inspection HUD

The round count and magazine pointer appear during Q inspection, while aiming down sights, and briefly after firing; they hide when the player is at rest. Press Q while the game canvas has keyboard focus to briefly show a north-up level map, live health and armor bars, measured horizontal movement speed in metres per second, fire mode, reserve ammunition, and four equipment slots. The map marker follows the player and points in the camera direction. The current scene only supplies a rifle, so unused slots are explicitly empty.

The map is generated in a dedicated worker from a horizontal slice of the level, by default 0.9144 m (three feet) above the detected floor, with a 0.4 m slice thickness and a 12 m radius. The worker uses the level's voxel octree when available. For levels without voxel files it decodes a bounded LOD of splat centers instead and estimates floor height from the player rig; that fallback is approximate. The map shows observed geometry, not guaranteed walkable space, and can contain gaps or scan noise. The slice is regenerated as the player moves or changes elevation. Very large requests are capped to keep worker time bounded. If the source data cannot be loaded, Q shows a compass and `MAP UNAVAILABLE`.

`InspectionHud.map_enabled`, `map_radius_m`, `map_height_m`, and `map_thickness_m` configure the map and are exported to the ECS inspector. Lua owns when to request and display maps; the worker performs the geometry query and returns a canvas texture. No map is generated continuously while Q is hidden.

Firing, aiming, reloading, equipping, changing fire mode or equipment, and other player combat actions immediately dismiss the Q-only stats and equipment HUD. The round count remains visible when firing or aiming. Incoming damage to the player also dismisses it. It never reopens when the action ends: release any held combat input and press Q again. Q refreshes the HUD's configured display duration, and remains reserved for inspection while the HUD is enabled. Escape opens the pause menu, where level selection remains available.

The round count appears above a fine line ending at the visible magazine. The line uses measured frame-space positions, the displayed footage frame, motion, clip blends, and the viewport transform. When the magazine is obscured or a footage clip has no reliable track, the line is hidden and the round count remains near its last tracked position; without one, it uses a stable position near the weapon, or the selected equipment slot during inspection. Screen-edge groups stay inside the game image when the viewport is letterboxed or integer-scaled.

Optional game systems can provide a player `Stamina` component with `current` and `maximum` to reveal the stamina bar, a player `Inventory` component with `active_slot` and a `slots` array of `{name, kind, count}` entries to populate equipment, and a `scene.objective` resource with `title` and optional `detail` to display an objective. Missing data is not fabricated. Other Lua systems can emit `engine.events.emit("combat.action", {player=player})` to dismiss the HUD for additional combat actions.

The UI is ordinary noninteractive ECS canvas geometry. `inspection_hud` owns its children and has an `InspectionHud` component with `enabled`, `duration_s`, `fade_in_s`, and `fade_out_s`, also exported to the entity inspector. `inspection_hud.lua` owns presentation and emits `player.inspect`, `hud.inspect_started`, and `hud.inspect_ended` through the reusable Lua event system. Other scripts can subscribe without coupling to the HUD. The separate `ammo_hud` layer follows the visibility rules even while `inspection_hud` is hidden. `InspectionHud.ammo_fire_seconds` controls how long the count remains visible after the trigger is released; holding either the physical or scripted trigger keeps it visible for hip fire and ADS fire. `first_person_controller.lua` publishes changed `hud.weapon_pose` values. The browser presentation host reports the installed frame while the callout is visible, so delayed image decoding cannot move the callout ahead of the image; the placement and visibility decisions remain in Lua. The gameplay scene’s `first_person_magazine` resource contains the measured pixel coordinates and frame size for each supported clip. Update those tracks when replacing the footage; clips without a track deliberately have no leader.

The values come from `WeaponState`, `Health` and `Armor` components. Health and armor use `current` and `maximum`; the player begins at 100 each. The Lua ballistics script applies `Armor.absorption` (0–1; default 0.5) until armor is depleted, and publishes the absorbed and health damage on `combat.damage`. Entities without armor retain their normal damage behavior.

Impact and collision diagnostic notices are visible only while the ECS tools are enabled with the backquote key.
